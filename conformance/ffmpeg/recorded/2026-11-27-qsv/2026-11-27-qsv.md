# Quick Sync recorded run, 2026-11-27 (S44 (Conversion), #169)

Recorded 2026-11-27 for S44 (Conversion), issue #169, before the build writes its ffmpeg pipelines. It records what jellyfin-ffmpeg 8.1.3-2 does on an Intel GPU with Quick Sync through D3D11VA and OpenCL tone mapping, and what its `p` key does while Plex transcodes on the same GPU.

**Result: the hardware chain works on every input, and the pause key works.** The one contradiction is colour range. Both Dolby Vision Profile 5 paths write full range output unless the filter is given `r=tv`. One copy saturates the GPU. A paused process keeps its GPU memory and uses no GPU time, and Plex keeps hardware transcoding next to it in both orders.

## Setup

- Jackson's Windows media server: Intel UHD Graphics 750 (8086:4c8a, Rocket Lake), driver 32.0.101.7085 dated 2026-03-02, i5-11600K, Windows 11 Pro build 26300.10075. The driver is outside the 31.0.101.5186 to 31.0.101.5534 range that gives green or blue frames when tone mapping, and newer than the 31.0.101.5590 fix.
- jellyfin-ffmpeg 8.1.3-2 portable `win64-clang-gpl` and dovi_tool 2.3.4, run from a scratch folder. The archive hashes are in `env.txt`.
- Clips from the `fakes/internal/mediafix/recipe.go` recipes, with its libx265 params, master display and RPU JSON, video only, in MKV: SDR H.264, HDR10, HLG, Dolby Vision Profile 5 and Profile 8.1. Each at 320x180 for 2 s and at 1920x1080 for 60 s.
- Every output was probed with the build's ffprobe: codec, profile, pixel format, the four colour fields, stream and first frame side data, duration and stream count.
- The chain, called chain A below, is Jellyfin's Windows QSV shape:

  ```
  -init_hw_device d3d11va=dx11:,vendor=0x8086 -init_hw_device qsv=qs@dx11 -init_hw_device opencl=ocl@dx11
  -filter_hw_device ocl -hwaccel d3d11va -hwaccel_output_format d3d11 -i <in>
  -vf hwmap=derive_device=opencl,tonemap_opencl=format=nv12:p=bt709:t=bt709:m=bt709:tonemap=bt2390:peak=100:desat=0,hwmap=derive_device=qsv:reverse=1
  -c:v h264_qsv -preset veryfast -b:v 4M -maxrate 6M -bufsize 8M
  ```

### The Profile 5 picture

I extracted one frame at 10 s from each output and compared it with `testsrc2` at the same frame.

- A and D show the right bars, a little desaturated by the tone map, with no tint. The recipe's 8.1 RPU is an identity, so D and A look the same.
- Both Profile 5 outputs have rotated hues, and they rotate differently. The hardware path gives olive, blue, cyan, maroon, red and magenta where `testsrc2` has red, green, yellow, blue, magenta and cyan. `tonemapx` gives green, blue, green, maroon, red and dark blue.
- **The clip is the cause, so this run can't judge Profile 5 colour.** The recipe's Profile 5 base layer is ordinary Y'CbCr from `format=yuv420p10le`, not IPT. Read as BT.2020 it shows the right bars. Applying the RPU's IPT matrix to it must rotate hues. A real Profile 5 source, or a recipe that writes IPT, is needed to judge colour. The run does show that both paths apply the RPU, since the output changes, and that they disagree with each other.
- `tonemapx` with `apply_dovi=0` on the Profile 5 clip gives a solid black frame.

## Speed (case E)

Chain A on the 1080p 60 s HDR10 clip, 1440 frames, copies started together.

| Copies | Each copy | Total |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 222 fps, 9.24x | 207 fps, 7.0 s |
| 2 | 107 fps, 4.47x | 206 fps, 14.0 s |
| 3 | 78 fps, 3.24x | 224 fps, 19.3 s |

Total throughput doesn't grow with copies. During one copy the GPU's 3D engine ran at 88 to 98 % and its video engine at 45 to 57 % (GPU Engine counters). The OpenCL tone map runs on the 3D engine, and that engine is the limit. **The default "Conversions at once" for this Quick Sync GPU is 1.** More copies only split the same throughput.

## Pause key (case F)

Chain A on the 1080p HDR10 clip, with stdin as a pipe and `-progress pipe:1 -nostats`. One copy finishes the clip in 7 s, so `p` went at about 4.5 s, not 10 s. The pause held for 20 s, then `u`.

- `p` works. stderr prints `Transcoding is paused. Press [u] to resume.` Frames already in flight finish within about 1 s (`out_time_us` from 34.3 s to 39.2 s), then `out_time_us` stays at 39166667 for the whole pause.
- While paused, CPU time grew 0.03 s in 20 s, and every GPU engine counter for the process read 0.
- **A paused process keeps its GPU memory.** `GPU Process Memory` Local Usage stayed at 610.9 MB throughout. Dedicated Usage reads 0 on this integrated GPU, which has no dedicated memory.
- `u` resumes, with no log line. The output probes as 1440 frames, 60.0 s, one stream, H.264 tv BT.709.

## Pause while Plex transcodes (case G)

Jackson played a film from Plex to a remote client with a video transcode. Plex Transcoder's arguments:

```
-codec:0 h264 -hwaccel:0 d3d11va -hwaccel_output_format:0 d3d11 -hwaccel_device:0 d3d11va ...
-init_hw_device d3d11va=d3d11va:0 -init_hw_device qsv=qsv@d3d11va -filter_hw_device qsv ...
-filter_complex [0:0]hwmap[0];[0]vpp_qsv=w=718:h=300:passthrough=0:format=nv12[1] ...
-codec:0 h264_qsv -b:0 1624k -maxrate:0 1624k -bufsize:0 3248k -preset:0 slower ... -f dash -seg_duration 8
```

This is hardware end to end: D3D11VA decode, `vpp_qsv` scaling and `h264_qsv`. Plex's last `Plex Transcoder Statistics.log` entry, from an earlier play, reads `transcodeHwDecoding="d3d11va" transcodeHwEncoding="qsv" transcodeHwFullPipeline="1"`. Plex throttles a transcode that is ahead of the player ("Going into sloth mode"), so it made one 8 s segment about every 8 s and the GPU in short bursts.

- **Conversion started while Plex ran.** I started chain A at 15:47:32, paused it at 15:47:37 and resumed it at 15:47:57. Plex's log shows segment ranges 57, 58, 59, 60 and 61 at 15:47:26, :34, :43, :47 and :58, the same pace as before. Its process kept its hardware arguments, and its GPU engine bursts (15 to 37 %) went on during the pause. The conversion finished full length.
- **Plex started while a conversion was paused.** I paused a conversion at 15:49:12, and Jackson seeked in the film, which started a new Plex Transcoder at 15:49:47 with the same hardware arguments. It ran at 45 to 50 % video engine and 48 to 54 % 3D engine for about 8 s to refill the buffer, then went back to bursts. The paused conversion held 613.9 MB of GPU memory throughout. I resumed it after 59 s, and it finished full length.
- A second, separate play wasn't started. The seek is a new transcode session on the same play.

## What this means for the build

- **Chain A as the spec has it works** on this GPU for HDR10, Profile 8.1 and Profile 5. No case ran HLG. Its clips were made but nothing converted them. The `hwmap=derive_device=qsv:reverse=1` shape is the one to write. The variant through `derive_device=d3d11va:reverse=1` fails.
- **Pass `r=tv` (or `range=tv`) to `tonemap_opencl` and `tonemapx`.** Without it a Profile 5 input comes out full range, and the predicted limited range output needs the option.
- **Never send a Profile 5 input to `tonemap_opencl` with `apply_dovi=0`.** ffmpeg aborts on an assertion rather than failing cleanly.
- **Set "Conversions at once" to 1 for a Quick Sync GPU of this class.** A second copy halves each copy's speed and adds nothing.
- **Pausing frees the GPU's engines and keeps its memory.** About 610 MB per paused 1080p conversion stays allocated. Plex still gets hardware next to a paused conversion on this machine. A GPU with less memory, or many paused conversions, may not.
- Don't offer AV1 encoding from `-encoders` alone. Check that the encoder opens.
- **The mediafix Profile 5 recipe isn't a picture fixture.** Its base layer isn't IPT, so a colour assertion on a Profile 5 output can't pass or fail meaningfully. It still exercises the Profile 5 code path and its metadata.
- The recipe's HDR10 clip carries its mastering display and light level only in SEI, not in the MKV stream's side data. `hevc_qsv` took them from the frames and wrote both into the output stream.

## Files

`2026-11-27-qsv/` holds:

- `results.json`, one entry per attempt: the case, the prediction, the command line, exit code, wall time, errors, the ffprobe facts and a verdict. It also holds the picture notes, the speed runs and the pause runs with their 2 s samples of `out_time_us`, CPU time, GPU engines and GPU memory.

The clips, outputs, frames and raw logs stayed in a scratch folder and were deleted after this summary. Plex's log lines were read for the transcode session only, with its token and client address left out. The film's path isn't recorded.
