# Design System: SlipStream

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Lit Console"**

SlipStream is a console lit by the Work it is showing. A Work's artwork sits blurred behind the page, and its hue runs through every surface and every ink, so a page about *Dune: Part Two* is a page in Dune's colour. Everything else holds still against that light. Red and amber are fixed alarms, green is a fixed all-clear, and live progress is white with a glow in the Work's hue. Glass blur belongs only to things that float. Lists, tables and cards are tinted translucent sheets with hairline edges, and nothing is drawn as a heavy box.

There is one theme, and it is dark. The Admin console is dense, built from A's layout in the direction ticket: a collapsible sidebar with live counts, a top bar with a ⌘K palette, and pages that open on a status sentence. The portal shares every token and component but runs at the Touch density, with B's poster-led layout and no machinery on screen. Geist sets everything. Geist Mono appears only in single values someone copies or compares down a column.

Motion is slow where light moves and quick where controls answer. The tint crossfades over 1.1s when focus moves to another Work. Buttons, switches and menus respond in about 200ms. Sheets and dialogs arrive on a spring.

**Key Characteristics:**
- The Work in focus colours the page through a blurred ambient layer and two CSS properties, a hue and a saturation.
- Fixed signal hues: red for failure, amber for warning, green for a passed check. None of them ever takes the tint.
- Live is luminance. A running job is white with the Work's glow, and Met is solid white.
- Translucent tinted surfaces, with glass blur only on floating chrome and overlays.
- Two densities: Console for the Admin desktop shell, Touch for the Admin phone shell and the whole portal.
- WCAG 2.2 AA is checked for every tint, and an ambient ceiling holds it.

## Colors

The palette is a tinted graphite whose hue follows the Work in focus, under three fixed signal hues and one solid white.

Every `hsl(214 …)` value in the frontmatter is the neutral resolution of a formula. Two registered CSS properties drive the tint: `--h` (the Work's hue, 0 to 360) and `--s` (its saturation, 18% to 70%). The frontmatter's `ambient.neutral` is their resting value. Inks keep their own saturation and take only the hue. Surfaces scale the ambient saturation. The canonical formulas follow, one row per tinted token, and `.impeccable/design.json` repeats them per token. The fixed signal colours (`fail*`, `warn*`, `ok*`, `solid`, `on-solid`) have no formula. `go run ./cmd/release gen tokens` writes the web theme from the frontmatter and this table, and `release lint` refuses a formula whose neutral resolution differs from its frontmatter value.

### Primary

- **Lit Graphite** (`page`, `glass`, `glass-raised`, `chrome`): The ground and the sheets laid on it. `page` paints under the ambient. `glass` carries tables, lists, the matrix and every card that is not a signal. `glass-raised` lifts a thing inside glass, such as a poster placeholder or a step disc. `chrome` is the sidebar and the bars.
- **Work Glow** (`glow`, `halo`, `live-fill`): The Work's own colour at full voice, and the only place it is bright. `glow` is the halo on live progress and the caret. `halo` is the outer focus ring. `live-fill` is the body of a chip that names something in flight.

### Secondary

- **Signal Red** (`fail` and its family): Failure only. `fail` is the solid chip, button, dot and failed stage segment, with `fail-deep` ink on it. `fail-card` is the near-opaque failure card, with `fail-text` for its copy, `fail-dim` for its secondary copy and `fail-ink` for its labels and for failure text set on glass. `fail-line` is the card's edge.
- **Signal Amber** (`warn` and its family): Warnings only, built the same way as red, with `warn-ink` for warning text set on glass. `warn-line` is the one amber hairline on amber callouts and amber rows.

### Tertiary

- **Signal Green** (`ok`, `ok-deep`): A check that passed, or a thing that is healthy. Examples are a Node online, a route hop passing, a provider key accepted, "Checked by Plex", Granted, a guardrail that allows a drop, Test connection passing and a seeding goal met. Green appears as a solid chip with `ok-deep` ink, a dot, or a check icon, always beside its words.

### Neutral

- **Ink tiers** (`ink-1` to `ink-4`): `ink-1` for primary text and values, `ink-2` for secondary text and unselected controls, `ink-3` for meta, counts and column headers. `ink-4` is not for text. It marks separators, chevrons, idle dots and disabled glyphs.
- **Fills and edges** (`fill`, `fill-hover`, `selected`, `hairline`, `hairline-strong`, `well`): Controls sit on `fill`. `selected` marks the chosen segment. Hairlines are 1px inset rings, never borders that change layout. `well` is the dark inset under inputs, code, attempt lists and machinery facts.
- **Solid White** (`solid`, `on-solid`): The primary action, Met, the toast and count badges. Dark `on-solid` ink always sits on it.

### The ambient

The ambient is a fixed layer behind each page, made of the Work's backdrop and a tint wash. The backdrop is blurred with `blur(64px) saturate(1.8)` and crossfades between two layers. The wash is two radial gradients at the Work's hue (26% and 22% lightness, alpha .55 and .45) over a vertical darkening at 4% to 5% lightness.

A sampler draws the poster onto a 24 × 36 canvas. It skips pixels with less than 4% chroma and weights each pixel by `sat² × (1 − |l − .5| × 1.6)`, which favours saturated mid-tones. It takes the circular mean of the hues, and sets `--s` to the weighted mean saturation × 80, clamped to 18% to 70%. A poster with no colourful pixels gives 220 / 12%.

Who sets the ambient:
- **A Work's page.** That Work.
- **Admin Home.** The most important job, meaning the job closest to Available, with a Member's request breaking ties. The pipeline sorts in that order, so the top row and the tint agree. In a desktop browser, hovering or focusing a pipeline row moves the tint to its Work after 90ms, and leaving returns it after 450ms. On phone, the topmost job in the middle band of the viewport sets it.
- **Portal Home.** The poster under the pointer or focus in a desktop browser, with the rest of the wall dimming. At rest, the first Work of the first shelf.
- **Any screen with no Work.** Nodes, Remote access, Settings, Providers and System use the neutral, hue 214 at 14%.
- **A failed Work never drives the tint.** Failure cards and rows carry no Work for the ambient.

### Named Rules

**The Ambient Ceiling Rule.** After its layers composite, the brightest region of the ambient stays at or below relative luminance 0.053. Text sits directly on the ambient (the status sentence, breadcrumbs, section counts), and 0.053 is the brightest ground on which `ink-3` still clears 4.5:1 at every hue. Before showing a backdrop, measure its brightest region in the 24 × 36 sample and set the image's `brightness()` to hold the ceiling. A fixed `brightness(.6)`, as the prototypes used, does not hold it: a white backdrop under that filter composites to about 0.16.

**The Fixed Signal Rule.** Red, amber and green never take the tint, and the tint never takes their meaning. A signal is a solid chip, a dot, a solid card (red and amber only) or an icon. Never a thin coloured word on a tinted ground.

**The Luminance Is Live Rule.** Running work is white with the Work's glow. Met is solid white. A hue on screen means trouble or a passed check, never motion.

**The Never Alone Rule.** Every signal colour comes with an icon and a word. Red and green are the pair most often confused, so colour alone never carries the state.

**The Contrast Proof Rule.** Change a colour token only if the contrast check in `go run ./cmd/release lint` still passes. It sweeps 120 hues at six saturations, finds the ambient ceiling, requires it to equal the frontmatter's `ambient.ceiling`, and checks 33 ink and surface pairs at it. It reads the same parsed tokens the theme is generated from, and its pairs, named by token, live in `cmd/release/internal/tokens/contrast.go`.

## Typography

**Display Font:** Geist (with Helvetica Neue, sans-serif)
**Body Font:** Geist (with Helvetica Neue, sans-serif)
**Label/Mono Font:** Geist Mono (with ui-monospace, monospace), for data only

**Character:** One grotesque at every size, tightened as it grows. Display and titles run at 700 with negative tracking. Body runs at 400 to 600 with none. Mono is a tool for values, not a voice.

### Hierarchy

The scale has ten sizes: 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 17, 20, 24, 34 and 56px. Geist and Geist Mono ship as bundled variable fonts (`@fontsource-variable/geist` and `@fontsource-variable/geist-mono`), and nothing loads a font from elsewhere. Use only the weights 400, 500, 600 and 700, never an in-between weight such as 650. `release gen tokens` refuses any other weight in the frontmatter.

- **Display** (700, 56px, 1.02): The portal's search hero headline and a Work's logo fallback on the portal.
- **Title large** (700, 34px, 1.15): The phone shell's large title.
- **Numeral** (600, 34px, 1, tabular): Big ordinals and headline figures, such as a provider's rank. Sans, never Mono.
- **Sentence** (600, 24px, 1.25): The desktop status sentence that opens Home, Torrents, Conversions and Remote access, and a page's title in the desktop shell. Also the portal's shelf titles and the wordmark on sign-in and setup.
- **Title** (700, 20px, 1.2): Phone section titles, the inspector title, dialog titles (a sheet's title too, since a Dialog becomes a sheet on phone) and the title of a sign-in or setup card.
- **Sentence touch** (600, 17px, 1.35): The phone status sentence, the phone nav bar's title, sheet items and a recommendation card's title.
- **Card title** (600, 15px, 1.3): A failure card's subject and a list row's title in Touch.
- **Body touch** (400, 15px, 1.45): Body copy at the Touch density.
- **Input touch** (400, 17px, 1.3): Text in Touch inputs. iOS zooms the page into any input set below 16px.
- **Heading** (600, 14px, 1.35): Section and panel headings in Console, and matrix row and column heads.
- **Body** (400, 13px, 1.45): Console body, table cells and buttons (600). Prose runs to 64ch at most.
- **Button** (600, 13px, 1): Button labels in Console. Touch buttons use Body touch at 600.
- **Meta** (400, 12px, 1.4): Secondary lines under titles, timestamps in prose and legends.
- **Chip** (600, 12px): Chips and badges. Signal chips are 700.
- **Label** (500, 11px, 1.3): Column headers, group labels, nav counts and the phone tab bar's labels.
- **Label caps** (600, 11px, 0.06em, uppercase): Only the labels of a failure card's facts. No other text is uppercase.
- **Data** (Geist Mono 400, 0.92em of its context, tabular): Values under the Copy or Compare Rule.

### Named Rules

**The Copy or Compare Rule.** Geist Mono sets a single value that someone copies or compares down a column: rates, sizes, durations, ETAs, percentages, clock times in tables, release names, file names, file specs, paths, hashes, keys, addresses and ports. Everything else is Geist. Counts inside sentences, section counts, ordinals, step numbers, weekday labels and ages are Geist with tabular numerals (`font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`). Apply Mono to the value's own element, never to a container that holds words.

**The Wall Clock Rule.** A time reads as the person's own clock reads it, in their locale's hour cycle, with its day only when it isn't today and its year only when it isn't this year. "6:36 AM" today, "Nov 19, 6:36 AM" on another day. A forced 24-hour clock and a date on every row are noise that clock doesn't show. A log's stamp is the one exception, 24-hour to the millisecond so its column keeps one width.

**The Age Rule.** A freshness fact reads as an age: Last seen, Last used, Last sign-in, Active, Read, Scanned, Measured, Started, Published and a Blocklist entry's Added. Every other past instant is a clock time, and a coming one says when it is due or how long is left. An age is words, never Data.

**The Log Row Exception.** A line in a Node's Logs section sets whole in Mono: its time, level chip, subsystem and message. The Admin reads a log down its columns like a table of values, so the row is the one container that sets in Mono. Its attributes, shown when the row expands, stay in Mono too.

**The Names Are Words Rule.** Names are never Mono. A Work, a Node, a Member, a Profile and a Target are names someone gave a thing, so they read as words, even when a Node's name looks like a hostname.

## Layout

The system has two shells and two densities. The shell follows the viewport and the density follows the audience.

### Shells

- **Desktop shell (768px and up).** Three columns: the sidebar, the content, and the Inspector's column when one is open. The top bar runs over the content and the Inspector's column.
  - **Sidebar.** 228px wide, collapsing to a 60px rail. It collapses on its own below 1100px, and the Admin can collapse it at any width with its button or ⌘K "Toggle sidebar". The choice is remembered per browser and holds at 1100px and up. Expanding the rail below 1100px is not remembered. The sidebar is `chrome` with a hairline on its right edge, and the mark (24px) and wordmark (17px) sit at its top beside the collapse button.
  - **Nav.** Items are 32px tall with 16px icons, `ink-2` until hovered (`fill`), and `selected` with `ink-1` at 500 when current. Activity carries a live count (a white glowing dot and a number), and other rows carry plain counts. A count shows only above zero and can carry an amber or red dot. Content Type rows nest under Library, indented 32px. The system rows, such as Nodes, sit below a separator. A row that lands on a page below its own, as Nodes lands on the Node the browser talks to, stays current on every page under it. On the rail, each row is its icon alone, with a Tooltip on the right carrying the label and count, and the live dot pinned to the icon's top right.
  - **Account.** The sidebar's foot is a 28px avatar with the Account's initial, the display name and "Admin". It opens a menu with Open the portal, the Startup sequence switch, Restart SlipStream… and Sign out.
  - **Top bar.** Sticky, 56px tall, laid out as `1fr minmax(320px, 440px) 1fr` with 28px sides. Breadcrumbs sit on the left in `ink-3`, with the current page in `ink-1` at 500. The ⌘K search capsule ("Search Works or run a command", ending with the platform's shortcut as a key hint) sits in the middle. Once the title scrolls under the 44px nav bar, the bar turns glass and takes the title in Sentence touch. Sections sit 28px apart with 20px titles, and content keeps 16px gutters.

A nav row's landing page is that destination's top page and takes the row's label, so a Node's page is titled Nodes and its pushed sections link back to Nodes. - **Safe areas.** Bars and sheets pad with `env(safe-area-inset-*)`, and the viewport meta carries `viewport-fit=cover`. Never hard-code the status bar or home indicator height.
- **Portal in a desktop browser.** A 64px top nav that sits transparent over the hero and turns to glass after 24px of scroll. ### Densities

The shell sets the density on the root as `data-density`, and components read its variables, so one component serves both densities. The portal is Touch on every device. Every other page follows the shell.

- **Console.** The Admin desktop shell. 13px body, 32px buttons, icon buttons and inputs (28px small buttons), 44px single-line rows, 60px two-line rows and 36px menu items.
- **Touch.** The Admin phone shell and the whole portal on any device. 15px body, 44px minimum targets, 50px primary buttons, 48px inputs, 54px single-line list rows, 64px two-line rows and 52px menu and sheet items.

### Rhythm

Spacing runs on a 4px grid (2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, 32), the same as Tailwind's default `--spacing`. Cards pad 16px. List rows pad 16px at the sides and take their height from the density. Sections sit 24px apart (28px on a Work page and on phone).

### Named Rules

**The Container Rule.** A component reflows by container queries, not by viewport breakpoints. The Torrents table drops to four columns at 980px and becomes stacked cards at 620px, wherever it is drawn. An open Inspector narrows the page, and the table answers without a breakpoint of its own.

## Elevation & Depth

Depth comes from translucency over the ambient, not from shadows. Surfaces at rest are flat tinted sheets edged by a 1px inset hairline. Shadows appear only under things that float, under posters, and under signal cards so they lift off the tint.

### Materials

- **Tinted translucent, no blur.** The sidebar (`chrome`), tables, lists, matrix, files, rails and board panels (`glass`), and things raised inside them (`glass-raised`).
- **Glass, blurred.** Only things that float.
  - The top bar, the phone tab bar, the nav bar and the status bar use `blur(24px) saturate(1.6)` over `chrome`. So does the portal's top nav once it turns to glass.
  - Menus, popovers, tooltips, the palette, the Inspector, Dialogs and sheets use `blur(30px) saturate(1.7)` over `popover` or `sheet`.
- **Solid.** Failure cards and callouts (`fail-card`, `warn-card` at .97), and every signal chip and button. Solid white on the primary action, Met, toasts and badges.
- **Artwork.** The blurred ambient behind every page. A sharp, edge-masked backdrop sits behind the header of a Work page and of the portal's Work page.

### Shadow Vocabulary

The frontmatter's `shadows` block holds each shadow's value.

- **Float** (`float`): Menus, popovers, a Select's list and tooltips.
- **Float large** (`float-large`): The palette, Dialogs and the Inspector.
- **Signal lift** (`signal-lift`): Failure cards, red callouts and red Signal rows. Amber callouts and amber Signal rows use `warn-line` as an inset ring with no drop.
- **Poster** (`poster`): Large posters and portal poster cards.
- **Toast** (`toast`).
- **Live glow** (`live-glow`): The fill of a live bar, the current stage segment, the bar on a selected row and the live dot. The live dot adds a 3px ring at `hsl(var(--h) 90% 65% / .32)`.
- **Segment lift** (`box-shadow: 0 1px 3px rgba(0,0,0,.28)`): The selected segment.
- **Focus** (`outline: 2px solid #fff; outline-offset: 2px; box-shadow: 0 0 0 6px var(--halo)`): Every focusable thing except menu items, sheet items and the palette input, which mark focus with `fill-hover` and a 2px glow underline. A selectable table row draws the outline inside its own edge, with no halo.

### Named Rules

**The Floating Glass Rule.** Blur means "this floats above the page". Nothing that scrolls with the content is blurred. That covers live pills and chips on posters too.

**The Reduce Transparency Rule.** Under `prefers-reduced-transparency: reduce`, every glass and translucent surface turns opaque at the same tint and lightness, and the ambient image hides, leaving the tint wash. Under `prefers-contrast: more`, `ink-2` and `ink-3` rise to `ink-1`, and hairlines rise to `hairline-strong` at full opacity. Under `forced-colors: active`, surfaces drop to system colours, and every state keeps its icon and its word.

## Shapes

Controls are capsules and containers are softly squared. A control a finger or pointer presses (a button, segmented control, switch, search field, toast or pill) is a capsule at 999px. Containers step up in radius as they float higher: 6px chips, 8px menu items, inputs and small art (row posters, stills and headshots), 14px cards, tables, menus and large posters, 20px Dialogs, and a 24px top edge on sheets. Marks smaller than 16px (state squares, stage segments, bars) round to a quarter of their height.

## Components

### Buttons

Capsules that press in.
- **Shape:** Capsule (999px). Console is 32px tall with 14px sides, and small is 28px with 12px sides. Touch is 44px with 18px sides and Body touch at 600, the primary is 50px, and a small button keeps the 44px target. A leading icon is 16px.
- **Default:** `fill` with a `hairline-strong` ring and `ink-1` text at 600. Hover turns `fill-hover`. Press scales to .97. Both transitions run 200ms on the standard ease.
- **Primary:** Solid white with `on-solid` ink and no ring. One per view.
- **Ghost:** Transparent with `ink-2`, turning `fill` and `ink-1` on hover.
- **Fail and Warn:** Solid `fail` or `warn` with deep ink, for the one fix a signal offers. Inside a failure card, the other buttons use `rgba(255,220,216,.1)` with a `.16` ring.
- **Disabled:** 40% opacity, no hover, no press.
- **Busy:** The label stays and a 14px spinner replaces the icon.
- **Icon buttons:** 32px circles in Console and 44px in Touch, with a 20px icon, `ink-2` until hovered, then `fill` and `ink-1`.

### Segmented control

An iOS-style choice of view or filter.
- **Track:** Capsule `fill` with a hairline ring and 2px padding. Options are 28px tall and 500 weight.
- **Selected:** `selected` fill with `ink-1` and the segment lift. Never solid white.
- **Counts:** Every option shows its count, All included, in tabular Geist.
- **Phone:** Full width with equal options, scrolling sideways when they overflow.
- **Semantics:** `role="radiogroup"`.

### Segmented links

The Segmented control's look over links, for a choice where each option is its own address, such as the Library's Content Type switch (All, then each enabled type in sidebar order). The row is a `nav` landmark named for the choice. Each option is a link, and the router marks the current one `aria-current="page"`, which takes the selected fill and lift. Counts are optional. A link is `ink-2`, turning `ink-1` on hover. On phone it spans the width with equal options and scrolls sideways, as the Segmented control does.
- **Library views.** "Library views" (Library, Wanted, Calendar) heads the Library, Wanted and the Calendar, before their Content Type links, and keeps the Content Type in view (`/library/music`, `/library/wanted/music`, `/library/calendar/music`). It carries no counts: Wanted's counts lead Wanted's own Status sentence. On phone it sits under the large title, or heads the content of a pushed page, with the Content Type links under it.

### Page tabs

The row of links under a page's title that moves between the page's sections, such as a Node's Overview, Web and API, Logs and Log settings. Each section is a route, so the row is a `nav` landmark named for the page's subject, holding links with `aria-current="page"`, never ARIA tabs.
- **Desktop:** Links are 36px tall in Body at 500, 24px apart, on a hairline that runs the row's width, with 24px below the row. A link is `ink-3`, turning `ink-1` on hover. The current link is `ink-1` with a 2px `glow` underline.
- **Phone:** No row. The page lists its sections as Touch rows with a chevron, and each section is a pushed page whose back link names the nav destination ("Nodes").

### Node header

Every page of one Node opens with its header: the Node's name as the page title, then on one wrapping line its state mark, "Last seen 3 hours ago" in Meta and `ink-3` after an `ink-4` dot while it is away, and its Role chips, outlined as on the Roster. Restart is the page's action. The header belongs to the Node's layout, so every section has it.
- **Desktop:** The line sits on the title's line, 24px after the name, and wraps under it in a narrow window. Restart is the top bar's action.
- **Phone:** The nav bar holds the Node's name on its top page and the section's on a pushed page, whose back link names the Node. The line and Restart head the content of every Node page, with Restart at the right.
- **Restarting:** The state reads Restarting with the live dot, Restart is disabled, and the section is hidden under "<name> is restarting…".

### Settings column

Settings has too many sections for one row of page tabs. Its sections sit in groups ([docs/settings-groups.md](docs/settings-groups.md)).
- **Desktop:** A column about 200px wide on the left of the page, sticky under the top bar, as a `nav` landmark named "Settings". Each group's label is Meta in `ink-3` above its links. A link is styled like a sidebar nav item, 32px tall, `ink-2` until hovered (`fill`), and `selected` with `ink-1` at 500 when current. A Health dot sits at a link's right.
- **Phone:** The Settings page lists one group of Touch rows per group, each under its label. Each section is a pushed page whose back link reads "Settings".

### Switch

A Console switch is 34 × 20 and a Touch switch is 51 × 31. The knob is white. The track is `hsl(var(--h) 20% 90% / .22)` off and `hsl(var(--h) 62% 46%)` on. The track fades over 250ms and the knob slides over 280ms. The whole labelled capsule is the switch target, and a sheet row with a switch is one target.
- **Mixed:** A switch over several things, such as a level's Monitor switch when some of its Items are monitored, can be mixed. Its knob sits centred on a track at `hsl(var(--h) 62% 46% / .55)`, and it reads `aria-checked="mixed"`. Pressing a mixed switch turns it on.
- **Row switch:** A row that a page draws by the hundred, such as an Item row, uses the row switch. It looks the same in every state and is a native `button role="switch"`, because one Base UI control per row made long lists slow to scroll.

### Checkbox and radio

A checkbox is a 16px square with a 4px radius (22px and 6px in Touch). A radio is a 16px circle (22px in Touch) with a white 6px dot (8px in Touch). Both sit on `well` with a `hairline-strong` ring and fill with the switch's on colour, `hsl(var(--h) 62% 46%)`, when checked. Each sits left of its label, and the label and box together are one target at the control height.
- **Radio cards:** A choice between services that have logos, such as the notification providers, is a grid of cards, each its 28px logo or icon and its name at 500 on `glass` with a hairline ring, radius 14. The chosen card takes `selected` and a 2px `glow` ring. The grid has three columns from a 448px container and two below it, and the arrow keys move the choice as in any radio group.

### Fields

- **Text field:** A label in Body at 600 and `ink-2` (Body touch in Touch), then a `well` input with a hairline ring, radius 8 and 12px sides, at the input height. Touch inputs set Input touch. Focus is the standard focus ring.
- **Description and error:** Meta in `ink-3` under the input (Body touch in Touch). An error replaces the description in `fail-ink` with its 14px alert icon.
- **Read-only value:** The same input set in Data, selecting all on focus, for a value someone copies. It can carry a default Copy button beside the input, which toasts "<Label> copied". The button shows only in a secure context, because the browser's clipboard needs one, and a Node on a LAN often answers plain http.
- **Secret field:** A saved secret that the server never sends back, such as an API key, a password or a feed's address. It reads "Saved" in `ink-2` in a `well` shaped like the input, followed by what may be shown of it in Data, such as the feed's host, with a Replace button beside it. Replace swaps in an empty input with Cancel, which returns to Saved. A save sends the secret only when a new one was typed. A secret not saved yet is a plain input. An optional secret also offers Remove beside Replace. After Remove the well reads "Removed. Save to apply." with Undo, and a save removes the secret.
- **Test line:** The outcome of a settings sheet's Test, under its fields and above Cancel, Test and Save. A pass is an `ok` signal icon with one sentence in `ink-1`, such as "The Test passed. Den sent the message.", then any warnings as `warn` lines. A failure is an alert with the `fail` signal icon and its sentence in `fail-ink`, and the field the failure names is marked "Change this, then test again." Details opens when that field is under it. The Download Client and notification connection sheets share it.
- **Text area:** The text field's label, `well` and ring over several lines, with 8px top and bottom padding and a vertical resize handle. Set in Data for pasted text such as a cookie or a definition file.
- **File field:** A label in Body at 600 and `ink-2` as a fieldset legend, then a default "Choose a file" button with a 16px upload icon, and the chosen file's name in Data in `ink-2` beside it. The file input itself is visually hidden and carries the label. Choosing a file hands it to the form, which reads it, such as filling a text area with a definition's YAML. The whole field is also a drop target: a file dragged over it rings the field in a 2px `glow` ring, and dropping it chooses it as the button would. The button stays the keyboard path.
- **Captcha:** A `glass` card with a hairline ring holding one sentence (whose sign-in needs the code, which Node fetched it, and when it expires at its clock time), the image on `solid` at up to 96px tall, then the Code field with a ghost "New captcha" button beside it. Once the attempt expires, the card holds the server's sentence as an alert and a default "New captcha" button instead.
- **PIN:** Four 40px slots (48px in Touch), 8px apart, masked and numeric.
- **Select:** A trigger shaped like the text field, with an up and down chevron in `ink-3`, opening a list styled as a menu with a check on the chosen item. An option can lead with a mark, such as a Node's lamp, and the trigger repeats the chosen option's mark. A mark that means something carries its word for a screen reader.
- **Setting field:** One setting an Admin can change: its label, its control, a Save that shows only while the draft differs, then hints in Meta (the default, the range, and "Applies after restart." for a setting the Node reads at start), and Reset to default when the value isn't the default. A refusal shows under the control in `fail-ink`. The control follows the setting's kind: a duration or count is an amount with its unit, a count whose 0 means none reads "No limit", a port is a number from 1 to 65535, a rate is an amount with a KB/s or MB/s Segmented control whose 0 reads its words such as "No limit", a size is an amount with an MB or GB Segmented control, a list is its entries as chips in one well, each with a remove button, then a text input that adds an entry on Enter, a comma or a space, an address is an Address and a Port field side by side, a folder is the Folder field on the setting's Node (its path in Data, Browse, and that Node's verdict for the folder's purpose), a choice is a Segmented control (a Select past three options), a switch is the Switch, and a window is two times with the zone. A setting fixed by an environment variable disables its control and shows a 14px lock with "Set by" and the variable in Data, and offers no Reset. In a list, a setting can sit as a row reading "<Label>: <value>" that opens the full field in a Popover, or a sheet on phone.
- **Search field:** A `fill` capsule with a hairline ring and a 16px search icon, at the control height.

### Chips and pills

- **Chip:** 24px, radius 6, `fill`, `ink-2`, 12px at 600. A Node chip is outlined.
- **Quality chip:** An outlined chip with the quality's name, such as WEBDL-1080p. Its name has the dotted `ink-4` underline of a technical state, and hovering, tapping or pressing Enter opens what its source means, such as WEB-DL or Remux. Inside a line of text it's the dotted name alone. Inside another control, such as a torrent row's button, it's the plain name, since a definition can't open inside a button.
- **Signal chips:** Solid `fail`, `warn` or `ok` with deep ink at 700 and a 14px icon. They name a state and its stage ("Failed at Import"), and they never take the tint.
- **Live chip:** `live-fill` with a hue ring and a white glowing dot.
- **Met pill:** Solid white capsule, 28px, with a check.
- **Node state:** A Node's state is a 6px lamp and its word in `ink-2`. The lamp is green for Online, amber for Reconnecting, Offline (inside the Offline grace) and On another version, red for Offline too long, and white with the Work's glow for Restarting. Reconnecting, Offline, Offline too long and On another version are technical states: the word has the dotted `ink-4` underline and opens its one-sentence definition on hover, tap and Enter. Inside another control, such as a Select option, a sheet item or a Roster row, the lamp carries its word for a screen reader and opens no definition.
- **Count:** A number in Label at `ink-3` with tabular numerals, optionally led by the live dot or a 6px amber or red dot. A screen reader hears the dot as a word.
- **Request stages (portal):** A Member sees a Request's stage (Pending, Approved, Searching, Downloading, Processing, Available or Denied) and the Delayed flag, as [Requests portal under Editions and Targets](https://github.com/jatassi/SlipStream/issues/88) decided and [ADR 0020](docs/adr/0020-a-request-stores-only-admin-decisions-and-derives-its-stage-from-the-work-tree.md) records. The Member portal's spec draws them. The Requested, Coming and Ready ribbons are retired.

### Status sentence

The first line of every page that watches live work. It reads as one sentence of counts and links ("5 jobs moving · 1 failure · relay-fra degraded"). It is set in Sentence type in the desktop shell and Sentence touch on phone, with tabular numerals, over the ambient with a soft text shadow (`0 1px 16px rgb(0 0 0 / .45)`). The live part leads with the live dot. Failure and warning parts are links to the cards below, in `fail-ink` and `warn`, each led by its signal icon. Separators are `ink-4`. A right-hand line of the day's totals shows in wide desktop windows.

### Stage track

A job's path from Searching to Available, one segment per stage (Searching, Downloading, Transfer, Import, Conversion, Available). Segments are 6px tall (8px on phone and in the Inspector) with 3px gaps.

A Grab's track keeps all six stops, and only the lit stop follows its stage. Downloading lights while the Grab fetches and downloads. Transfer lights while any file of its Import is sending from another Node, and Import lights for the rest of the Import. A failed Grab lights the stop of the stage it failed at. A Grab's Conversion stop reads Not needed. An imported Grab shows the quiet Imported mark (a tick in `ink-3`) in place of its word and track, so the work still moving stands out.

The stage word over a track has the dotted underline of a defined word and opens the track's legend on hover, tap and Enter, a detail Popover named "Stages of" and the Work. It lists the six stops, each with its segment in its look, the stop's name in `ink-1` and the segment's word in `ink-3`: Done, In progress, Not started, Not needed or Failed. On phone, and in a full failure card, the track names its ends, Searching under its left end and Available under its right, in Meta at `ink-3`.
- **Done:** Flat `ink-2`.
- **Current:** `ink-1` with the live glow.
- **Ahead:** `fill`.
- **Not needed:** A `hairline-strong` outline at 45% width.
- **Failed:** Solid `fail`.

Running tracks are `role="progressbar"` with a stage-and-title label. Others are `role="img"`.

### Progress

There are two kinds, and every bar maps to one. Work that is moving is Progress (`ProgressBar` and `ProgressCell` in `web/src/ui/progress.tsx`), a `progressbar`. A quantity against a limit is a Meter, a `meter`.
- **Live.** A white fill rising from the Work's hue, with the live glow. A sheen sweeps it every 2.4s. Width moves linearly each second. The track is a 6px capsule at `hsl(var(--h) 60% 80% / .14)` with a hue ring. Used for downloads, Conversions, Transfers, task runs, library scans, re-home checks and live matrix cells.
  - **Settled.** The same 6px capsule on `fill` with a flat `ink-2` fill, no ring, glow or sheen, and no width motion. Used for a download that is paused or reads an old sample, so its row keeps its height, and for the top bar's tasks capsule, whose live dot already says the run moves.
  - **Progress cell:** The amount on the left in Meta at `ink-1`, the percent in Data unless the work counts something else ("4 of 12 steps"), and a note on the right in `ink-3` that truncates, such as the pace and time left, the bytes, when it started or its Nodes. The bar sits 6px under them. It is at most 240px wide in a table cell and 360px in a line, and fills a list row's meta.
- **Meter.** Flat `ink-2` on a 4px `fill` capsule, with no glow and no sheen. Used for storage, provider allowances and seeding goals.
  - **Seeding goal.** The lane meter: two lanes, ratio and seeding time, 4px each in tables and 8px in the Inspector, where each lane's name and value sit above it. A lane runs to its goal, or to the Tracker's rule when the rule asks more, and a lane past its end is full. A 2px `ink-1` tick, taller than the lane, marks the rule and turns `warn` while a hit and run is at risk. A lane the rule doesn't count has no tick. A goal short of the rule is a 2px `ink-3` mark. A lane neither the rule nor the goal counts is an empty `hairline-strong` outline. The amber tick never stands alone: an at-risk row carries the amber "At risk" chip, and a met goal the green "Goal met" chip. A Forever goal's lanes run to the rule, and the row then reads "Seeds with no end". Each lane is a `meter` whose value text names its rule and goal.
  - **Allowance.** Thin by design.
- **Spinner.** One spinner: 14px, a white arc on a faint ring, turning every 0.8s.

### Failure card

The answer to "what broke". A near-opaque red-black card (`fail-card`, radius 14, signal lift). Its header row is the signal chip with the failed stage, the time in Mono `fail-dim`, and the actions on the right (the fix as a Fail button first). Its body puts the Work and its stage track beside its facts. The labels are Label caps in `fail-ink` (Cause, Recovery, Release), and values run to 78ch.
- **Full:** Home's lead failures and Torrents' problems, which tile at `minmax(360px, 1fr)`. The subject and its track take a third of the body and its facts the rest.
- **Compact:** The Work page's Needs you list. It drops the stage track, and its actions sit in a row at the bottom right.
- **Phone:** A separate 16px-radius card with each fact's label over its value and two actions (the fix and More).

### Signal row

The compact red or amber row for lists that can grow long: the Conversion queue's Needs you band, problems inside a table, and Needs you rows past the first few cards. It is one line of chevron, signal chip, subject (600), a two-line clamped verdict and the time, padded 12px by 16px with radius 14. On phone, the chevron, chip and time share the first line, and the subject and verdict wrap below. It expands in place to the full detail, such as each attempt with its Node, time and error in a `well`. Enter or Space toggles it, with `aria-expanded`. The chevron turns over 250ms.

### Callout

A condition the person weighs before going on, in amber or red (`Callout` in `web/src/ui/callout.tsx`). A callout is the signal's card, `warn-card` with a `warn-line` hairline or `fail-card` with the signal lift, at radius 14 with 12px by 16px padding. It holds the signal chip that names the condition, then the sentence, a "since" time in Data when the condition has a start, and at most one fix as a Warn or Fail button. It is a status, so a screen reader reads it as it appears. A callout names a condition and a Signal line reports what was just done. If no word fits the chip, it is a Signal line.
- **Condition:** Inside a form, sheet or dialog, such as Slow pattern in the Custom Format editor or Many sources after a Torznab Test. The chip and the sentence, usually with no button, because the form's own Save or Grab is the decision. In a narrow frame the sentence wraps under the chip.
- **Standing:** On a page, such as Restart needed, Port taken or a Settings section's worst Health item. A bold subject leads and the detail follows in the dim ink, then the time and the fix. A red callout may add a failure card's other buttons in `on-fail` after its fix, as Torrents' client cards add Edit client.
- **Phone:** A standing callout is a 44px row of the chip, the subject and a chevron. It opens a sheet titled with the subject that holds the card and one full-width button per action, and an action closes the sheet first. A condition stays in place.

### Signal line

One sentence led by its signal's icon, beside the control whose outcome it reports (`SignalLine` in `web/src/ui/signal-line.tsx`), such as a refused save, a Test that passed with its warnings, or a Node that can't be reached. The icon is in the signal's hue (`fail`, `warn` or `ok`), the words are in `fail-ink`, `warn-ink` or `ink-1`, and the line takes the text size around it. It is announced only when it reports what was just done, as an alert for a failure and a status otherwise, so a list of five failing Indexers isn't five alerts. A field's error under its input belongs to Fields, and a tinted word in a row's cell is that row's state.

### Empty

What a list, table or section shows when it has nothing (`Empty` in `web/src/ui/empty.tsx`). One sentence in `ink-2`, at most 64ch, says what is missing and then what to do or what SlipStream does next. An optional way forward follows it, as one button, a button group or a link. In Console the action sits after the sentence when both fit on its line. In Touch it sits under the sentence. An empty has no title and no icon, because its sentence already leads with what is missing.

### Time

An instant on screen (`RelativeTime` and `PlainTime` in `web/src/ui/relative-time.tsx`, worded by the forms table in `web/src/ui/time-words.ts`). Every time reads one shared clock that turns on the wall minute, each second only while something counts seconds, and not at all while the tab is hidden, so the ages and countdowns on a page change together. A time is in the person's zone, except one that answers to a setting stated in another zone, such as the Tasks window in the Controller's, which adds the person's own clock to its moment.
- **Standing:** A time on its own, such as a Fact's value, a stage's time or a callout's since, is `RelativeTime`. Hovering, tapping or pressing Enter opens its whole moment, "Saturday, November 21, 2026 at 2:02:00 PM UTC".
- **In a row:** A time inside a link, a button or a row that opens is `PlainTime`, with its whole moment as its title, since nothing can open inside them.
- **In a sentence:** Words with their preposition, through `whenWords`: "at 2:02 PM", "yesterday at 2:02 PM", "on Mon at 2:02 PM". It has no hover, because the sentence names the day.
- **Forms:** Clock ("2:02 PM" today, "Nov 19, 2:02 PM" on another day), Time (the hour alone, under a day heading), Stamp (a log line's "14:02:37.123"), Due ("Tomorrow at 4:00 AM"), Countdown ("In 25 min") and Age ("12 minutes ago"). Clock, Time and Stamp are Data where someone compares them down a column. Due, Countdown and Age are words.

### Key hint

The key that triggers a control, as one keycap at its trailing edge (`Kbd` in `web/src/ui/kbd.tsx`, read from the key table there). The keycap is `fill` at radius 6 with 2px by 6px padding, in Label at `ink-2`. On Apple platforms a chord prints as glyphs in ⌃⌥⇧⌘ order, such as ⌘K or ⇧⌘N. Elsewhere its names join with +, such as Ctrl+K. Screen readers skip the keycap, because the control carries the same keys in `aria-keyshortcuts`, and Touch shows none.
- **Capsule:** The top bar's search capsule ends with the palette's shortcut.
- **Footer:** The palette footer, one keycap per key with 2px between them, then the word: ↑ ↓ Move, ↵ Open, Esc Close.
- **Tooltip:** After a tooltip's words, for a control whose key works where the person is, such as the Diagram's zoom buttons, whose -, + and 0 work while the Diagram has focus.

### Route

Remote access draws each Exposure as a lane of hops, in the order a player's connection crosses them. Its route kind sets the hops:
- **Direct:** Player, DNS, TLS, Origin and Media Server. An Unplaced route, one whose public name resolves inside the home, draws the same hops.
- **Through your own proxy:** Player, DNS, Proxy, TLS, Origin and Media Server. The Proxy hop is the Admin's own server at the public address, which SlipStream doesn't run, so its checks read what a player sees there and the origin's bind and firewall are facts.
- **Through the Relay Node:** Player, DNS, Relay, TLS, Node link, Origin and Media Server.

The UI and portal is SlipStream on the origin, so its route ends at Origin, with no Media Server hop. A route's fix shows in the page's card and in its hops' checks, never again under its hops.

A hop is a small `fill` tile joined by a 2px connector, and it takes the worst result of its checks. A failing hop takes the signal card of the verdict its failing checks point to, red for Needs a relay and amber for Fixable, with a 3px connector after it in that signal. A hop past a failure reads "not reached", with a dashed outline and a dashed connector. A passing hop carries a green check. A Not tested or Not used hop stays `fill` with no signal and says so under its name, "not tested" or "not used". A Not tested hop also takes the outline of the Not tested chip, and a Not used hop the dim ink. Selecting a hop opens its checks in the Inspector in the desktop shell and a sheet on phone. Each check leads with its result and family, then what it means and its fix, with what was seen and the Node that saw it, and when, under Details.

A route that is Reachable and was tested from outside folds to one line: its subject, its public address, its families tag, its route kind and the green Reachable chip. Every other route draws its hops.

The verdict chip names the verdict, Reachable, Fixable or Needs a relay, and takes signal colour only from a failing check (the Fixed Signal Rule): red for Needs a relay and amber for Fixable when a failing check decides it, green for Reachable once tested from outside, and the neutral chip otherwise. A route not tested from outside in every family it offers carries an outlined "Not tested" chip beside the verdict, so an untested route never looks like an alarm. Each chip's word opens its one-sentence definition.

The page leads with one recommendation card, mapped from the verdict that decides it:
- **Needs a relay on a failing check:** a red standing Callout with the chip "Needs a relay", the subject and the fix, and no button until a Relay Node can be set up from here.
- **Fixable on a failing check:** an amber standing Callout with the chip "Fixable", the subject, the fix and the fix's button as its Warn action, such as Turn on Open router ports automatically.
- **Resting only on Not tested:** a neutral glass line with the subject, the step and a ghost "Add a Node" button.
- **Every route Reachable and tested:** no card.

Each Role's machine has a fixed place, the same on every tile: the tall ones along the back and left, and the small ones, each about a third of the piece board's floor, in two rows in front of them, clear of the chips. Capsules travel along it at a rate set by the link's throughput, capped by what its round trip can carry, and faster on a quicker link. The end that dialed glows. A down link is empty.
- **Lettering.** Plaques and chips are engraved in Geist Mono, as labels on equipment are. It is the one place mono sets words, because the engraving spaces every letter on one advance and is part of the drawing, not data.
- **Reading it.** Hovering a part, or the idle tour when no pointer moves, lights it and dims the rest. A click or the arrow keys select a Node, a Role or a link and open the Inspector, which says it in words with the machinery under Details. On phone the Inspector is a sheet. Escape clears.

- **Zoom and pan.** The fitted view is the least the Diagram shows, and it zooms to five times that. The wheel or a trackpad pinch zooms about the pointer. Dragging anything but a Role chip pans a zoomed Diagram, and a press that moved selects nothing. Zoom out, Zoom in and Fit sit in a glass capsule at the Diagram's top right, and +, - and 0 do the same while it has focus. A button or key eases the change over 220ms, and the wheel and a drag follow the hand. A Role moved keeps the view, and a Node added or removed fits it again.

- **Motion.** Fills and capsules ease toward each traffic sample over the second until the next. Under reduced motion the Diagram is one still frame: capsules spaced along each link at its rate and every board at its fill. A zoom jumps to its view.

The Roster says everything the Diagram draws, in rows, and a screen reader reads the Roster.

### Logo

The mark is Two Nodes: a stream S whose two ends are dots, one for each kind of Node, the Controller and the Relay. It is drawn on a 64-unit grid. The S is one stroke, `M40 14H25a9 9 0 0 0 0 18h14a9 9 0 0 1 0 18H24`, 8 units wide with round caps and joins. The dots are 5.5-unit circles at (52, 14) and (12, 50). The masters live in `brand/`.

- **In the app.** The mark is inline SVG. The S takes `ink-1` and the dots take `glow`, so the dots follow the Work in focus like every other light on the page.

The manifest's colours and the `theme-color` meta are `page`'s neutral, `#0c0d0e`.

The Relay answers, the neutral ambient blooms, and the mark slides left as the wordmark wipes out from behind it. It uses the neutral blue, because no Work is in focus yet.
  - **Master.** `brand/startup/sequence.js` is the master. It exports the duration, the phase table and `render(t, stage)`. The web app imports it, so the app and the master cannot drift, and it draws with the bundled Geist and `brand/startup/grain.png` from the Node. `brand/startup/startup.html` plays the same module on its own, and `brand/startup/render.mjs` renders `startup.mp4` from that page frame by frame.

### Motion

- **Easing.** The standard ease is `cubic-bezier(.16, 1, .3, 1)`, and the spring is `cubic-bezier(.32, .72, 0, 1)`.
- **Ambient.** The tint (`--h`, `--s`) and the backdrop crossfade over 1.1s on the standard ease. The incoming backdrop settles from 104% scale over 1.6s.
- **Controls.** Buttons, segments and switches respond in 200 to 280ms. Disclosure chevrons rotate over 250ms. Sections open and close at once, with no height animation. The phone nav bar and the portal's top nav turn to glass over 200ms.
- **Overlays.** Menus and tooltips take 180ms, the palette and toasts 220ms, the Inspector 280ms, sheets 340ms in and 260ms out, and Dialogs 360ms.
- **Shell.** The sidebar collapses and expands over 280ms on the standard ease. A page change in the desktop shell crossfades the content alone over 180ms. On phone, a push slides the new page in from the right over 340ms on the spring while the old page drifts 30% left and dims to 60%, and a pop reverses it. The bars, the sidebar and the tab bar hold still through both. In an installed web app, a drag of 80px or more from the screen's left 20px goes back.
- **Live.** Fills move linearly once a second, and the sheen loops every 2.4s. The spinner turns every 0.8s.
- **Artwork.** An image fades in over 200ms on the standard ease once it loads. The hero backdrop fades over the ambient's 1.1s. An image the browser has already decoded shows at once.
- **Loading.** Skeleton bars breathe between `fill` and `fill-hover` over 2.4s on the standard ease, slower than any live motion, so a skeleton reads as waiting. A skeleton appears after 300ms with a 180ms fade, so data that lands sooner never flashes one.
- **Startup.** The sequence runs 4.4s and leaves over 360ms, as the Logo section says.

The frontmatter's `motion` block holds these easings and the overlay durations. `release gen tokens` writes them to the theme as `--ease-*` and `--duration-*`.

**The Reduce Motion Rule.** Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, the tint and backdrop swap at once, images appear without their fade, fills jump without the sheen, and overlays appear without travel. Page transitions and the sidebar cut. Spinners, steps, skeleton bars, the PIN shake and canvas animations stop, and a skeleton still waits 300ms but appears without the fade. No animation is exempt.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** take every surface and ink from the formulas in Colors, so a Work's hue reaches the whole page.
- **Do** hold the ambient at or below relative luminance 0.053, measured per backdrop.
- **Do** run `go run ./cmd/release lint` after changing any colour token, and keep its contrast check passing.
- **Do** lead every watching page with a status sentence, then failures with their stage, cause and recovery, then live work.
- **Do** show live work as white with the Work's glow, and Met as solid white.
- **Do** pair every signal colour with an icon and a word.
- **Do** use capsules for every control, at 32px in Console and 44px or more in Touch.
- **Do** blur only what floats: bars, menus, popovers, the palette, the Inspector, Dialogs and sheets.
- **Do** set rates, sizes, ETAs, clock times in tables, release names, paths and keys in Geist Mono, and everything else in Geist.
- **Do** use the neutral ambient (214 / 14%) on every screen without a Work.
- **Do** pad bars and sheets with `env(safe-area-inset-*)`.

### Don't:
- **Don't** draw a light theme. v2 has one dark theme (parity row P-012 is excepted). Emails are the only light surface.
- **Don't** tint red, amber or green, and don't let a failed Work drive the ambient.
- **Don't** use green for Met, for live work or for a solid card. Green marks a passed check or a healthy thing, as a chip, dot or icon.
- **Don't** add a fourth red. `fail-ink` covers failure text on glass, including the status sentence's link.
- **Don't** set a name, a sentence, a count in prose or a step number in Geist Mono.
- **Don't** use solid white for a selected segment or tab. Solid white means the primary action or Met.
- **Don't** blur anything that scrolls with the content, such as a live pill or a chip on a poster.
- **Don't** put text over a sharp backdrop unless a mask or scrim holds the ambient ceiling beneath it.
- **Don't** use `ink-4` for text.
- **Don't** use uppercase outside a failure card's fact labels.
- **Don't** use B's condensed display face (Big Shoulders Display). Geist sets the portal too.
- **Don't** show Members any machinery: no Nodes, Targets, stages, releases or Conversions on the portal.
